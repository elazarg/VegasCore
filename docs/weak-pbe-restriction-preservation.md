# Weak PBE preservation across a clean action restriction

Analysis by Codex. The statement below is a **paper theorem with a mathematical
proof**, not a checked Lean capstone. It concerns a structural restriction
between finite extensive games. It does not establish that an arbitrary
blockchain runtime realizes the restriction or its collection law, and it does
not change the asynchronous checklist or semantics.

The result is stronger than what follows immediately from the checked SE
extension theorem: the source assessment may be an arbitrary weak PBE, whose
off-path beliefs need not be the limit of any common tremble sequence.

## Games, beliefs, and operational assumptions

Let H and R be finite extensive games with the same finite player set and
perfect recall. Chance kernels and legal action menus are fixed. Information
sets contain legal histories, including histories unreachable under a selected
strategy. Every legal action menu is finite and nonempty. A behavioral policy
assigns an action lottery at each of the player's information sets.

Here **weak PBE** means an assessment `(sigma, mu)` satisfying both:

1. At every decision information set I, the prescribed whole continuation
   policy maximizes expected terminal utility conditional on mu(I), against
   the other players' prescribed policies. Alternatives include changing all
   of the player's later choices, not just its current action.
2. At every information set with positive reach probability, mu(I) is the
   Bayesian conditional distribution of its actual histories. At a zero-reach
   set, mu(I) may be any probability distribution supported on that set.

No relation between distinct off-path beliefs is assumed. This is the weak
notion used here, rather than a stronger PBE definition imposing additional
off-path updating conditions.

Let `r : H -> R` be a structural action restriction, of the kind represented by
`InformationModel.ActionRestriction`. It embeds source histories, information
values, and legal choices; preserves initial history, activity, terminality,
observations, and history lengths; and commutes with each retained transition.
Call an embedded source decision information set a **copied site**. Every
other raw decision information set is a **new site**. Histories generated by
retained actions are called clean.

The clean history embedding preserves prefixes. Along a clean history, every
owned decision information set and action is the embedded source information
set and retained action. These are consequences of the structural transition
and observation clauses, rather than assumptions about an equilibrium. In
particular, a clean initialized execution with embedded source choices stays
within the history image. A nonimage history cannot later become an image
history: every prefix of an embedded history is itself embedded.

Write `u_i(h)` for source terminal payoff and `b_i(t)` for raw base payoff.
A fixed terminal service reads an available observation and samples a verdict
identifying whose escrow is actually collected. Let `a_i(t)` be player i's
collection probability conditional on terminal history t. The expected raw
utility is

\[
  v_i(t)=b_i(t)-D_i a_i(t).
\]

The audit has no intervening strategic reporter or player decision. Its random
verdict defines the actual realized payoff vector, not just an alarm. Assume:

1. Every clean embedded source terminal history matches base payoff and is
   audit-clean: `b_i(r(h))=u_i(h)` and `a_i(r(h))=0`.
2. There are finite bounds `u_i(h)>=L_i` and `b_i(t)<=U_i` for all legal terminal
   histories.
3. For every player i, copied site I, hidden source history h in I, and raw
   action a excluded from I's retained menu, committing a from r(h) leads to
   actual collection with conditional probability at least `rho_i>0`, against
   **every** remaining raw continuation profile. This includes all later
   timing, communication, and attempts to recover or avoid the charge.
4. Deposits are fixed nonnegative finite numbers with
   `rho_i D_i >= U_i-L_i`.

Assumption 3 is a pointwise runtime collection law. It does not assume desired
beliefs, incentive comparisons, or an equilibrium. It is nevertheless a
substantial backend obligation: escrow, admissible evidence, attribution, and
actual collection need an implementation proof. Visibility of pending traffic
does not itself supply that proof.

## Preservation statement

**Weak PBE restriction preservation, paper theorem.** Under these assumptions,
every weak PBE `(sigma, mu)` of H has a weak PBE `(tau, nu)` of R such that:

- At every copied site, tau is the embedded sigma lottery and nu is the
  embedded source belief mu.
- The initialized raw terminal-history law is exactly the embedded source
  law.
- Initialized raw play collects no deposits, and the joint law of terminal
  history and realized settlement payoff is the embedded source payoff law.

One deposit vector works for every source weak PBE. For example, choose

\[
 D_i=\max\{0,(U_i-L_i)/\rho_i\}
\]

before selecting the source assessment. The result is forward preservation;
it does not characterize all raw weak PBE or prove a source compiler/runtime
refinement by itself.

## No return to a copied site after a new owned decision

Fix a player i and a raw history at a new site J. Once i acts at J, its
remembered own play contains the information/action entry for J. Every clean
history contains only copied entries in that player's own play.

Suppose a later history were at a copied site I for player i. That copied
information set also contains a clean embedded source history. Perfect recall
would require the two histories to have the same remembered own decisions and
actions. The first history remembers J, while the clean history has no such
entry, a contradiction.

Therefore, after acting at a new site, a player can never again act at one of
its own copied sites. Other players may still act at copied sites. The claim
does not say that all later decisions are new for everyone.

This observation is essential. It lets us solve new-site continuation policies
without allowing that player to change future copied choices. A generic
one-shot principle with arbitrarily incompatible off-path beliefs would not
justify that step.

## Constructing rational continuation play at new sites

For each copied site I, choose a full-support lottery q_I over **all raw
actions** at that site, including excluded actions. Choose positive
`0 < epsilon_n <= 1` with `epsilon_n -> 0`, and set

\[
 \alpha_n(I)=(1-\epsilon_n)r_*\sigma(I)+\epsilon_n q_I.
\]

For each n, form a finite auxiliary game R_n. At copied sites, replace the
player's decision with a chance draw using alpha_n(I). Keep its action result
and observations in the raw history. At new sites, keep the original player's
action menu and information partition. Terminal utilities are v_i.

Full support over all raw actions matters: freezing only retained menus would
remove every excluded-action ingress and could erase the new sites from the
auxiliary game's legal history tree. With alpha_n, every original legal raw
history remains legal in R_n. The histories at new sites therefore correspond
to the same raw information fibers at each n.

Replacing some decisions by chance preserves perfect recall for the remaining
decisions. Equality of the original information states preserves the player's
past new decisions and actions; the sampled copied actions remain observations
in the history. Thus R_n is a finite perfect-recall game and has an SE. Let
beta_n be its strategies at new sites and eta_n its beliefs there.

Interpret beta_n together with the frozen alpha_n as a raw profile tau_n.
At a new site J, any raw whole-policy deviation by the acting player has
exactly the same continuation effect as a deviation in R_n. After the current
new decision, the no-return result prevents that player from encountering an
own copied site. Opponents' copied choices have the same alpha_n laws in both
interpretations. Hence the SE of R_n supplies **whole-policy sequential
rationality** at every raw new site, conditional on eta_n(J).

Take one joint convergent subsequence of beta_n and eta_n over all new sites.
There are finitely many strategy and belief simplices, so such a subsequence
exists. Its raw strategy limit tau uses embedded sigma at copied sites; write
eta for its new-site belief limits.

For a fixed new site and any fixed whole-policy deviation, expected continuation
utility is continuous in the finite strategy and belief coordinates. The
whole-policy optimality inequalities for tau_n therefore pass to the limit.
This proves whole-policy rationality of tau at each new site under eta.

No source consistency sequence was used. The source sigma is enough to choose
alpha_n, and epsilon_n can vanish at any positive rate. The source mu will be
installed separately.

## Installing copied beliefs and Bayesian beliefs on initialized play

Set nu(J)=eta(J) at new sites. At every copied site I, set nu(I)=r_*mu(I),
with support on its clean embedded histories. This replacement does not change
the strategy profile or any new site's belief. Continuation utility at a site
depends on the strategy and that site's own starting belief, not on the belief
labels attached to later sites. New-site rationality is therefore unchanged.

With embedded source policies at all copied sites, initialized raw execution
stays clean and reproduces the source history law. Every new site has zero
reach probability. A copied site's positive reach histories are exactly the
embedded source histories with their original weights. Consequently its
Bayesian conditional belief is the embedded source Bayesian belief. Since the
source weak PBE obeys Bayes' rule wherever reached, nu obeys the required raw
Bayes condition.

At zero-reach copied sites, the installed mu may be incompatible with the
limiting eta elsewhere. Weak PBE permits that incompatibility. The construction
does not claim KW consistency of the resulting assessment.

## Whole-policy comparisons at copied sites

Fix a copied source information set I for player i, and start with its
installed belief `r_*mu(I)`. Fix an arbitrary raw continuation deviation pi_i.
The other players use tau. We must compare pi_i with tau over its entire
remaining continuation, rather than infer the result from arbitrary-belief
one-shot optimality.

First represent pi_i as a distribution over complete deterministic,
observation-local raw continuation plans p. One direct construction presamples
an action at every relevant own information set according to pi_i. Perfect
recall implies that an own decision information set cannot be revisited along
one legal history, so this presampling reproduces the behavioral continuation
law from each starting hidden history. It also reproduces that law after
averaging over mu(I). Chance and the other players' behavior are unchanged.

For each pure raw plan p, construct a pure lawful source continuation f_p:
follow p's intended choices at copied source sites while they are retained;
at the first excluded intended choice, replace it with a fixed lawful pure
source fallback, and use that fallback thereafter.

The first-departure flag is observation-local. The player knows the fixed plan
p. Source perfect recall remembers its own past source information sets and
actions. At a later source information set, inspect those own sites **after
the starting site I** to determine whether p prescribed an excluded choice
earlier. The intended choice is determined by p, even if its replacement
fallback action equals an otherwise retained action. Before I the deviation
has not started; choices prescribed by p there do not set the flag. At source
information values unreachable from I, define any lawful fallback choice.

This defines an ordinary pure source policy, not an omniscient repair selected
after seeing the hidden history. No unobserved behavioral coin flag is needed.
Equivalently, a repair with a privately remembered random flag could be
behavioralized using standard perfect-recall mixed/behavioral equivalence;
the pure-plan construction avoids that extra step.

Couple the raw play of p and the source play of f_p, starting from the same
draw h from mu(I), with identical chance and opponent draws until the first
excluded action. Before that departure all histories and action distributions
match under r, because opponents play embedded sigma at clean copied sites.

On a path with no departure, terminal utility agrees exactly. Conditional on
any first departure at a hidden clean history and excluded action, the robust
collection law and the base-payoff upper bound give

\[
 \mathbb E[v_i\mid\text{that first departure}]
 \le U_i-\rho_iD_i\le L_i.
\]

The corresponding source repair continuation has payoff at least L_i on every
terminal outcome. Its expected conditional payoff is therefore at least L_i.
The collection bound covers the entire remaining raw behavior of p and the
other players, including their new-site play. No compliance after departure
is assumed. Combining departure and no-departure branches yields

\[
 V_R(p\mid r_*\mu(I),\tau_{-i})
 \le V_H(f_p\mid\mu(I),\sigma_{-i}).
\]

Source weak-PBE whole-policy rationality bounds the right side by the source
prescribed continuation value at I. That prescribed value equals the raw
prescribed continuation value, since clean play commutes and collects nothing.
Finally average over the pure-plan mixture realizing pi_i. Every pure repaired
policy has already been bounded by the same prescribed value. Thus no mixture
realization of the repairs is needed to conclude

\[
 V_R(\pi_i\mid\nu(I),\tau_{-i})
 \le V_R(\tau_i\mid\nu(I),\tau_{-i}).
\]

This establishes whole-policy rationality at every copied site, including
source off-path sites with arbitrary compatible mu(I).

Together with new-site rationality and the reached Bayes condition, this proves
that `(tau,nu)` is a weak PBE. Clean initialized play gives the terminal law.
Zero collection probability for every player on clean histories makes the
actual joint settlement law equal to the original source payoff vector.

## Scope and formalization gap

The mathematical construction uses ordinary finite-game SE existence only for
the auxiliary new-site games. It does not assume a target equilibrium, source
KW consistency, or desired copied-site comparisons as a backend premise.

It still requires a faithful structural clean interface. If a legitimate
source continuation discloses additional hidden information in the raw game,
the restriction premise can fail; weak PBE does not repair that defect. The
honest-disclosure counterexample in the
[general analysis](general-se-preservation.md) therefore remains applicable.

The formal SE capstone in
[TerminalAudit](../GameTheoryExtensions/Analysis/Protocol/TerminalAudit.lean)
is unchanged. It proves forward preservation of source SE, not this arbitrary
source weak-PBE theorem. Checking the latter requires adapters for the
no-return property, freezing copied lotteries into chance while retaining raw
histories, rational new-site limits, and the observation-local first-departure
repair. Existing finite-game existence, compactness, continuation, and
enforcement APIs provide the underlying ingredients; these adapters have not
been assembled into a Lean proof.

Finally, this is a theorem for the specified finite-game weak PBE definition.
It does not assert preservation for stronger PBE notions that constrain
off-path updating across the copied and new sites, nor for infinite action or
history models without further existence, compactness, and integrability work.
